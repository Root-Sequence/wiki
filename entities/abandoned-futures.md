# Abandoned Futures

## Short definition

A materially or socially developed possibility that advanced beyond pure speculation, could have supported a meaningfully different trajectory, was interrupted or displaced, and remains recoverable through evidence, surviving practice, infrastructure, memory, or renewed development.

The category is diagnostic rather than celebratory. An abandoned future may have been useful, harmful, exclusionary, brittle, authoritarian, ecologically destructive, technically unsound, or some mixture of these. **Abandoned does not mean worthy.**

## Why it matters

Present systems often make one historical outcome look inevitable after alternatives have disappeared from ordinary memory. Abandoned-futures work restores contingency by asking what actually existed, what future it implied, why it ended, who held veto power, what harms it carried, and what—if anything—remains worth repairing or transforming.

This keeps three questions separate:

1. Was the possibility technically or socially viable enough to leave evidence?
2. Why did it fail to become durable?
3. Should any part of it be recovered under present conditions?

A path can be recoverable without being desirable, and desirable without being recoverable in its original form.

## Boundaries

The framework is explicitly not nostalgia, technological determinism, or a presumption that capitalism explains every failed possibility. Historical reconstruction should distinguish promise from demonstrated capacity, identify competing explanations, surface excluded groups and hidden labor, and state uncertainty.

Recovery may require different ownership, governance, accessibility, ecological limits, privacy protections, maintenance models, or refusal of the original project's hierarchy. The useful object is the **possibility**, not automatic restoration of the system that once contained it.

## Ecosystem use

- **Root Sequence:** owns the public historical and systems framework and evidence discipline.
- **Being Human(e):** asks how lived demonstrations and historical contingency make alternatives emotionally and practically imaginable.
- **Community Infrastructure:** can test bounded recovered mechanisms against current governance, maintenance, safety, access, and participation constraints.
- **Coherent World / No One Noticed:** may transform selected possibilities into speculative-design or narrative work without turning research evidence directly into canon.
- **Museum of Ordinary Life:** can preserve ordinary evidence and provenance before later histories flatten partial experiments into inevitable progress or total failure.

Private project-specific applications and canon remain in the private layer rather than being copied here.
