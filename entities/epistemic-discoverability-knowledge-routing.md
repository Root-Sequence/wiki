# Epistemic Discoverability and Knowledge Routing

## Short definition

A developing design concept for whether a knowledge environment helps people move from the questions and vocabulary they already possess toward relevant concepts, fields, methods, communities, sources, and terminology they do not yet know how to name.

**Knowledge routing** is the companion move: helping a question reach the field, method, source, community, or vocabulary best equipped to develop it without forcing every inquiry through one ontology or authority.

## Why it matters

Knowledge can be public, searchable, and technically available while remaining practically inaccessible to someone who does not yet know the canonical term, relevant discipline, source type, prerequisite concept, or neighboring vocabulary.

The concept treats vocabulary, source paths, representations, field maps, aliases, disagreements, and provenance as part of knowledge infrastructure rather than assuming a correct query already exists.

A durable design objective is therefore to let inquiry evolve visibly: new terms can change the question, new fields can change the search path, and partial understanding can expose better next questions.

## Relationships

- **Legible Systems:** practical understanding should remain possible without expert vocabulary while deeper source and mechanism layers stay reachable.
- **Root Sequence Thought Routing:** one inquiry can move toward an appropriate canonical home without collapsing distinct domains into one explanation.
- **Cognitive On-Ramp:** people need small, current entry points before they can navigate a project's deeper structure.
- **User-Configurable Cognitive Interfaces:** different people may need different routes through the same knowledge space.
- **Legible AI-Assisted Expression:** AI-supported synthesis should preserve sources, transformations, uncertainty, and the route for continued independent inquiry.
- **Root Sequence Design Grammar:** “make knowledge discoverable before vocabulary is mastered” is now an explicit recurring design move.

Root Sequence **Reading Trails** are now a concrete bidirectional implementation pattern for this concept: a project question can lead to a trail, while wandering a trail can surface a new project question. The September 25 routing guide keeps source evidence separate from local adoption and routes checked distinctions into bounded inquiries, project changes, tests, corrections, or explicit non-adoption. Their substantive trail content remains in the research repository rather than being duplicated here.

## Boundaries

Epistemic discoverability is not maximum information exposure, a claim that one ranking is correct, a replacement for libraries or disciplinary methods, or a reason to hide disagreement behind a smoother concept graph.

More information can reduce discoverability through overload. A useful route should balance relevance, novelty, cognitive load, uncertainty, provenance, accessibility, serendipity, primary-source access, and the person's ability to stop when they know enough for the current purpose.

AI can reduce vocabulary barriers, but it can also become a gatekeeper by selecting one interpretation too early, summarizing away disagreements, or making itself the only path to source material. The design goal is expanded epistemic agency, not permanent dependence on the router.
