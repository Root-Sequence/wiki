# Agency and Automation

## Short definition

A cross-project design lens for distinguishing helpful automation from loss of agency by asking which burdens can be removed while preserving meaningful ways to participate, delegate, refuse, inspect, contest, recover, and change course.

Its provisional shorthand is:

> **Automate necessity. Preserve possibility.**

The phrase is not a rule that all necessary work should be automated or that manual participation is inherently better. The canonical treatment instead distinguishes unwanted friction, chosen effort, protective pauses, bounded delegation, affected-party authority, recovery, and the distribution of labor and gains.

## Core distinctions

Agency can include doing something directly, receiving assistance, accepting a suggestion, delegating a bounded action, or relying on shared background infrastructure. Those modes are not a maturity ladder. Initiation, observation, execution, authorization, review, and recovery are separate dimensions, and the appropriate arrangement depends on the activity, stakes, affected parties, and ability to reverse or repair consequences.

A person's permission to automate their own work does not automatically authorize decisions, surveillance, commitments, or harms for other people. Automation should therefore be evaluated alongside authority, consent, fallback, maintenance, ownership, governance, and who absorbs hidden correction work.

## Boundaries

The concept does not claim that automation automatically creates free time, material security, equality, or meaningful work. Those outcomes depend on ownership, governance, transition costs, ecological limits, maintenance, bargaining power, access to essentials, and the distribution of gains and losses.

The public source is explicitly an AI-assisted working principle awaiting author review. Its modes, safeguards, and compact wording are proposals rather than empirically validated universal rules.
