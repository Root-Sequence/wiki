# Root Sequence Wiki

A living map of the ideas, language, projects, and relationships that make up Root Sequence.

Use the Wiki to orient yourself, follow an idea across projects, or find the canonical place where the fuller work lives. It is a navigation and context layer—not a replacement for the projects themselves.

## Explore the Wiki

- **[Projects](PROJECTS.md)** — meet the projects and find their canonical homes.
- **[Entities](entities/README.md)** — browse durable concepts, motifs, and other shared identities.
- **[Project lenses](https://wiki.rootsequence.systems/lenses/)** — see the entities and references gathered around one project, without duplicating them.
- **[Lexicon](LEXICON.md)** — look up recurring Root Sequence language.
- **[Phrases & motifs](PHRASES.md)** — revisit lines and patterns that have become conceptual tools.
- **[Knowledge graph](https://wiki.rootsequence.systems/graph/)** — move through explicit connections among projects, entities, terms, and phrases.

Not sure where to begin? Start with **[Projects](PROJECTS.md)** for the broad landscape, then follow a project lens or a linked entity wherever your curiosity leads.

## One Wiki, many paths

An idea has one identity here even when it appears in several projects. Indexes, project lenses, search results, and the graph are different paths through the same knowledge—not separate copies competing to be authoritative.

Substantive arguments, designs, research, stories, and implementations remain in their canonical project repositories. The Wiki keeps those homes findable and shows how their ideas relate.

## Public knowledge, private depth

This site is the public layer of the Root Sequence Wiki. Private material lives in a separate overlay that can add context without becoming a source for the public build.

The details are documented in **[Wiki Architecture](ARCHITECTURE.md)** and **[Public + Private Layers](PRIVATE_OVERLAY.md)**. Editorial and operational material is collected under **About** in the site navigation.

## What belongs here

The Wiki helps answer:

- What is this called?
- Which projects use it?
- Where does the substantive work live?
- What is connected to it?
- How has it changed over time?

If you need the complete argument, design, implementation, or canon, follow the canonical project link from here.
